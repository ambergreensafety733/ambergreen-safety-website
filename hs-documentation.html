H&S Documentation

Safety Management Systems and the Health & Safety documentation needed to support them.

What is a Safety Management System?

A Safety Management System (SMS) is the organised framework a business uses to manage Health & Safety. It brings together your policies, procedures, risk assessments, safe working methods, inspections, training, accident arrangements and records so that Health & Safety is actively managed rather than simply documented.

The documentation required should be proportionate to the size of the business, its activities and the risks involved. You do not need a mountain of paperwork; you need the right arrangements, communicated to the people who need them and kept under review.

The Main Health & Safety Policy

The main Health & Safety Policy sets out the organisation's commitment to Health & Safety, who has responsibility for managing it and the arrangements used to control workplace risks. Where required by law, it should be documented, brought to employees' attention and reviewed when circumstances change.

Other Health & Safety Policies

Depending on the business and its activities, additional policies may be appropriate. Examples include policies covering workplace safety, occupational health, drugs and alcohol, lone working, driving for work, work equipment, environmental matters or specific higher-risk activities.

These policies should explain the organisation's approach and responsibilities rather than simply copying generic wording that does not reflect the business.

Procedures

Procedures explain how particular Health & Safety arrangements are put into practice. They may cover subjects such as consultation, training, reporting hazards, inspections, maintenance, contractor control, emergency arrangements, document control and incident management.

Risk Assessments

Risk assessments identify hazards, who may be harmed, the level of risk and the measures required to control it. They should be suitable and sufficient, kept current and reviewed when there is a significant change or when experience shows that controls need improvement.

Safe Working Methods

Safe Working Methods, Method Statements and RAMS explain how particular tasks are to be carried out safely. They turn the findings of risk assessments into practical instructions for the people doing the work.

For construction and higher-risk work, they can be particularly important in demonstrating that risks have been considered and that suitable controls are communicated before work starts.

Auditing & Inspection Documentation

Inspections provide a check on workplace conditions and whether required controls are being followed. Audits look more broadly at whether the Health & Safety management arrangements themselves are working effectively.

Documentation can include inspection checklists, audit reports, action plans, follow-up records and evidence that corrective actions have been completed.

Accident, Incident & Near-Miss Reporting

Businesses should have arrangements for reporting and recording accidents, incidents, dangerous occurrences and near misses. The process should identify what happened, ensure appropriate action is taken and, where necessary, investigate the underlying causes so lessons can be learned.

Where an incident falls within the requirements of RIDDOR, the organisation must also consider whether it is reportable and follow the applicable reporting requirements.

Specific Health & Safety Templates

Some subjects require their own assessment, records or control arrangements because of the particular hazards involved. Ambergreen Safety can help prepare or review templates appropriate to your activities, including:

COSHH

Control of Substances Hazardous to Health arrangements should identify hazardous substances, assess the risks of exposure and set out appropriate controls. Supporting information can include COSHH assessments, substance inventories, safety data information, storage arrangements and monitoring records where required.

Asbestos

Where asbestos may be present, businesses need appropriate arrangements to identify and manage the risk. Depending on the premises and work involved, documentation may include an asbestos management plan, asbestos register, information for contractors and records of surveys or remedial work.

Work at Height

Work at height should be planned and assessed, with suitable equipment, competent people and arrangements to prevent falls. Documentation may include risk assessments, method statements, equipment inspection records and rescue arrangements where appropriate.

Manual Handling

Manual handling assessments should consider the task, load, working environment and individual capability, with suitable measures to avoid or reduce the risk of injury.

Work Equipment

Businesses using work equipment need arrangements for selecting suitable equipment, providing information and training, maintenance and inspection where required. Records should demonstrate that equipment is being properly managed.

Fire Safety

Fire safety arrangements should reflect the premises and activities and include appropriate fire risk assessment arrangements, emergency procedures, maintenance and testing records and staff information or training.

Other Specific Subjects

Depending on your activities, documentation may also be needed for subjects such as lone working, workplace transport, lifting operations, electrical safety, noise, vibration, confined spaces, first aid, emergency arrangements and contractor management.

Keeping Documentation Under Control

Good documentation should be controlled, accessible and kept up to date. Obsolete documents should not continue to be used, reviews should be recorded and employees should know where to find the information relevant to their work.
